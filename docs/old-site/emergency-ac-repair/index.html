When It Cannot Wait

A dead air conditioner in a Dallas summer is not an inconvenience — it is a safety problem. Indoor temperatures above 90 degrees put children, elderly family members, and pets at risk of heat-related illness within hours. Waiting until Monday is not always an option.

What Usually Fails

Most emergency AC failures in Dallas come down to five things:

  • ⚡

    Blown capacitor. The most common summer failure. The capacitor stores the charge that starts the compressor and fan motors. When it fails, the unit hums but does not start — or starts and shuts off within seconds. A 15-minute repair on most systems.

  • ⚡

    Locked compressor. The compressor overheats and trips on its internal safety. Sometimes it resets after cooling down; sometimes the windings are burned and the compressor needs replacement. The technician tests it on site and tells you which one you are dealing with before quoting anything.

  • ⚡

    Frozen evaporator coil. Low refrigerant, restricted airflow, or a failing blower motor causes ice to build on the indoor coil. The system blows warm air or stops blowing entirely. The fix depends on the root cause — a dirty filter is a $0 fix; a refrigerant leak is a repair.

  • ⚡

    Failed contactor. The contactor is the switch that sends power to the compressor. When it welds shut or burns out, the system either runs constantly or will not start at all. Another common, inexpensive part.

  • ⚡

    Tripped breaker or blown fuse. Sometimes the fix is the simplest one. The technician checks the electrical panel and the disconnect before diagnosing anything else, because a $0 breaker reset beats a $400 repair every time.

What to Do While You Wait

Turn the thermostat to OFF — not just a higher temperature, but completely off. Letting the system keep cycling when something is wrong can turn a simple repair into a compressor replacement. If you hear grinding, screeching, or a loud buzz, turn the system off at the breaker.

Check your air filter. A clogged filter can cause a system to overheat and shut down on safety, and replacing it sometimes restores function immediately. Pull the filter out, hold it up to a light — if you cannot see light through it, replace it.

Close all blinds and curtains, especially on south- and west-facing windows. In a Dallas summer, direct sunlight through a window can raise the room temperature several degrees per hour. Keeping the sun out buys you time.
