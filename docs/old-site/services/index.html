Repair or replace: how the decision usually goes

Most systems that stop cooling can be fixed. Capacitors, contactors, fan motors and clogged drains account for a large share of Dallas service calls, and none of them justify a new system. The replacement conversation belongs to a narrower set of cases: a failed compressor on an older unit, a large refrigerant leak in a coil, or a system well into its second decade that is costing more every summer.

If you are handed a replacement quote, it is reasonable to ask why the system cannot be repaired, what the repair would cost, and how the new system was sized. A good technician will answer all three without pressure. You can also ask for a second opinion. Call and say that is what you want.

A note on refrigerant and replacement quotes

You may have been told your system has to be replaced because R-410A refrigerant is being phased out. The federal rules changed in 2026, and a working R-410A system does not have to be replaced because of the refrigerant. Parts like compressors and coils can still be repaired. Our free homeowner guide walks through what changed and what to ask before you sign a replacement contract.
