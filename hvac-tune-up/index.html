Tune-Ups

Fix it in April, not in August

A system that runs seven months a year in Dallas heat wears out small parts steadily. A check before each season is the cheapest way to find them while the repair is still small and the schedule is still open.

When to book

Book the cooling check in March or April, before the first 90-degree week, and the heating check in October, before the first norther. Those are the quiet months for HVAC companies, which means easier scheduling and a technician who is not racing to the next emergency.

Wait until the first heat wave and you are competing with every system in the metroplex that failed the same week.

What a proper tune-up includes

A tune-up is only worth what is actually checked. Ask for a written checklist, and expect something close to this:

Spring: cooling

  • Clean and inspect the outdoor coil
  • Test the capacitor and contactor, two of the most common summer failures
  • Check refrigerant pressures and temperatures against the manufacturer’s range
  • Clear the condensate drain line and check the pan
  • Check blower operation and airflow
  • Tighten electrical connections and check the thermostat

Fall: heating

  • Inspect and clean the burners and flame sensor
  • Test the ignitor and ignition sequence
  • Inspect the heat exchanger for cracks
  • Check venting and test for carbon monoxide
  • Verify gas pressure and safety switches
  • On heat pumps, check defrost and backup heat

A word on refrigerant. A healthy system does not use up refrigerant. If a technician says yours is low, the real question is where it is leaking. Topping it up every year without finding the leak is paying for the same problem over and over.

What you can do between visits

Change the air filter every one to three months, more often with pets or during heavy use. Keep grass clippings, leaves and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor unit. Make sure supply and return vents are not blocked by furniture or rugs. If you notice ice on the copper lines, water around the indoor unit or the system running without stopping, turn it off and call.

Be careful with

Pressure to replace a system during a routine tune-up, a list of urgent repairs with no explanation of what failed and why, and yearly refrigerant top-ups. It is always reasonable to ask to see the worn part, to get the diagnosis in writing, and to get a second opinion before paying for anything major.
