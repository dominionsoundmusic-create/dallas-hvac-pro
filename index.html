Why AC Breaks Down in Dallas

Dallas runs air conditioning seven months a year. From June through September, the metroplex averages highs between 96 and 107 degrees, and systems run nearly continuously for four straight months. The equipment lives where the heat is worst — in the attic, where temperatures reach 140 to 160 degrees even when the outdoor air is only 100.

That kind of sustained load wears out capacitors, burns out compressor windings, and pushes refrigerant systems past their design limits. The homes most at risk are pre-1980 construction — and Dallas has a lot of it — with undersized ductwork, poor insulation, and systems that were never designed for the loads modern Texas summers demand.

We carry the parts that fail most often — capacitors, contactors, fan motors, and control boards — on every truck. Most repairs are finished in one visit. When the system is beyond repair, we size the replacement correctly for your home and your ductwork, not just the tonnage the old unit happened to be.

The 2026 Refrigerant Changeover

If your system runs on R-410A, it still works and it is still legal to service. But R-410A production is being phased down under the AIM Act, and prices are rising. New systems now ship with R-454B, which is more efficient but requires different tools and training.

What this means for you: repair your current system as long as it makes economic sense. When repair costs approach half the price of a new system, or when R-410A refrigerant costs make a recharge uneconomical, that is when replacement pays for itself. We will tell you which side of that line your system is on — honestly, not as a sales pitch.
