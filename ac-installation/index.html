AC Installation

A new AC, sized for your house

A replacement system is one of the biggest checks most Dallas homeowners ever write for their house. Here is what should happen before anyone quotes you a price, and what to ask so the new system actually fits the home it is going into.

Start with a load calculation, not the old unit’s size

The most common installation mistake has nothing to do with brands. It is sizing the new system by copying the tonnage of the old one. Plenty of Dallas homes, especially older ones, were fitted decades ago with systems far larger than they need, and a rule of thumb applied again today repeats the error.

The right starting point is a room-by-room load calculation, known in the trade as a Manual J. It accounts for your square footage, insulation, windows, which way the house faces and how tight it is. An oversized system cools the air quickly and shuts off before it removes the humidity, which leaves the house cold and clammy and wears the equipment out with constant starts and stops.

If a quote gives you a tonnage without explaining how it was reached, ask. A good installer will be glad you did.

What has changed in 2026

Efficiency minimums are law. In Texas and the rest of the southern region, a new home-sized central air conditioner must meet at least 14.3 SEER2 (larger units have a slightly lower floor). Be wary of anyone offering a suspiciously cheap system that falls below it. It cannot legally be installed as new equipment here.

New refrigerants. Most new residential systems now run on R-454B or R-32 rather than R-410A. Both are established refrigerants and both work well. You may not get a choice, since it follows the equipment brand. What matters more is the quality of the installation.

You do not have to replace a working R-410A system. Federal rules changed in 2026, and an existing system can keep running and keep being repaired. If you are being pushed to replace a system that works, read our free refrigerant changeover guide first.

The federal tax credit is gone. The 25C credit that many quotes still mention ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025. Utility and state rebates may still apply, so ask the installer what is available.

What drives the price

Installed prices vary widely from one company to the next, even for the same house, which is why a second quote is worth the time. The main things that move the number are the size of the system, the efficiency tier you choose, whether the indoor coil and furnace or air handler are replaced at the same time, and the condition of the ductwork.

Going from the minimum efficiency to a mid-range system usually cuts electricity use noticeably for a moderate premium. The gains flatten out above that. Variable-speed systems earn their keep mostly by handling humidity better, which matters in a Dallas summer.

Before you sign, ask

  • How did you calculate the size, and can I see it?
  • Does this meet the 14.3 SEER2 minimum, and what is its actual rating?
  • Are the indoor coil and outdoor unit a matched pair?
  • Will you pull the permit and arrange the inspection?
  • What does the labor warranty cover, and for how long, as well as the parts warranty?
  • Did you check the ductwork, and does it need sealing?
