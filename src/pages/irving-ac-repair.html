Irving: from a 1903 lot sale to Las Colinas

Irving started with a lot auction on 80 acres of ranch land in December 1903 and incorporated in 1914. It stayed a small farm community until after World War II. Then it grew faster than almost anywhere in Dallas County: 2,615 residents in 1950, 45,895 in 1960 and 100,996 in 1970, according to the Texas State Historical Association. The Dallas Cowboys moved to Texas Stadium in Irving in 1971, and the stadium was demolished in 2010.

The 1970s also brought DFW International Airport along the city's western edge and the start of Las Colinas, a planned community on the north side that now covers more than 12,000 acres of offices, apartments and homes around Lake Carolyn. Valley Ranch followed as another master-planned area. The 2020 census counted 256,684 people across about 67 square miles. So an Irving AC call might come from a 1960s house in south Irving, a 1990s two-story near Valley Ranch, or a high-rise apartment in Las Colinas.

What Irving's rental rules mean for a broken AC

Irving inspects its apartment complexes on a schedule. The city says multifamily properties are inspected at least once a year depending on their risk rating, and the highest-risk properties at least twice. Tenants with an unresolved maintenance problem are told to put it in writing to management, let inspectors into the unit to document it, and avoid filing anonymously, because anonymous complaints are hard to act on.

State law sets the larger frame. Under the Texas Property Code, a landlord must make a diligent effort to repair a condition that materially affects the health or safety of an ordinary tenant, once the tenant gives notice and is current on rent. The code mentions cooling directly in one place: when a landlord has agreed to provide heating or cooling equipment, the equipment is producing inadequate heat or cooled air, and a local housing, building or health official has notified the landlord in writing, the tenant gains additional repair remedies. Legal aid groups can explain the details for a specific situation.

Permits, power and gas in Irving

In Irving, electrical, mechanical and plumbing work each need their own permit, applied for online through the city's MGO permit portal. Mechanical permits do not need plans. The Inspections Department, at City Hall on West Irving Boulevard, answers at (972) 721-2371. The city makes a point worth repeating: when a contractor signs the permit, it agrees to do the work to code, and if you sign it yourself, you are the one responsible for fixing work that fails inspection. Permits generally expire 180 days after they are issued if work has not started.

Before you call: safe checks for an Irving home or unit

  • Thermostat: in Cool mode, set lower than the room, batteries fresh.
  • Breaker: a tripped AC breaker may be reset once. If it trips again, stop.
  • Filter: in apartments the return grille is often in a hallway ceiling or wall. A dirty filter there can freeze the coil.
  • Outdoor unit: on a house, clear leaves and clutter from around it. In an apartment, note which unit is yours if you know.
  • Water: a puddle under the closet unit, or a system that shut itself off when its drain backed up, should be described on the call.
