Lewisville: a farm town the lake turned into a suburb

According to the Handbook of Texas, Lewisville began as Hallford Prairie, took the name of Basdeal W. Lewis after he bought the land in 1853, and built the first cotton gin in Denton County in 1867. It stayed small for a long time: about 1,500 residents in 1950. Federal engineers started impounding Lewisville Lake on the Elm Fork of the Trinity River in November 1954 and finished the project in 1955. With the lake, Interstate 35E and later DFW Airport, the town took off, reaching 24,273 people in 1980, 77,737 in 2000 and 111,822 in 2020.

The lake still shapes the city. The Texas Water Development Board lists its conservation pool at 27,175 acres and describes it mainly as a water supply for Dallas, Denton and nearby communities, and Wikipedia notes that about 6.2 of the city's 43.2 square miles are water. That steady climb means Lewisville has houses from every decade since the 1960s, plus a large share of apartments. The Census Bureau puts the owner-occupied rate at 46.3 percent, so more than half of homes are rented. When the AC fails in a rental, who calls whom matters, and the city has a clear rule about it, covered below.

Oncor or TNMP? Sorting out power in Lewisville

Lewisville is served by more than one transmission and distribution utility. Texas-New Mexico Power, known as TNMP, is headquartered here and serves much of the city, and some addresses are on Oncor's lines instead. Your wires company is printed on your bill. You still choose your own retail provider either way.

The house is dark

That is an outage. Report it to the wires company on your bill: TNMP at 888-866-7456, or Oncor. Turn the thermostat to Off until the power is back and steady.

Lights on, AC off

Make sure the thermostat says Cool and has fresh batteries. A tripped breaker can be reset once; a second trip means stop and get an HVAC company out.

Running but warm

Replace a dirty filter and clear grass or debris from around the outdoor unit. Ice on the lines or a fan that does not spin means shut it off and call.

The city's HVAC rules, including for rentals

The City of Lewisville's Building Services Division publishes a residential HVAC handout, updated January 1, 2024. It says a permit is required for all HVAC installations, which must meet the International Residential Code, the International Mechanical Code and local ordinances. Applications are filed online and fees are paid before the permit is issued.

The handout spells out who may do the work. A homeowner may work on his or her own primary residence under state law, but anyone else must be a licensed mechanical contractor registered with the city. Recovering refrigerant always takes a licensed HVAC contractor. And on rental property, the city says all work must be done by a licensed HVAC contractor, with no exceptions.

  • If you rent: report the problem to your landlord or manager in writing and keep a copy. Your lease normally says how repair requests must be made and who arranges the work.
  • If you own a rental: a licensed contractor is required for the work, and the permit is part of the job. Ask for both on the quote.
  • If you own your home: let the contractor pull the permit and schedule the inspection, and keep the record for when you sell.

Castle Hills joined the city in 2021

Castle Hills, northeast of the original city, was a large development outside Lewisville's limits for years. The city completed its annexation on November 15, 2021, adding almost 3,000 acres and about 18,000 residents. That is part of why the Census Bureau's estimate for Lewisville jumped to 139,006 by July 2025.

For Castle Hills homeowners, annexation means the City of Lewisville's permit and inspection process now applies to HVAC replacements. The city's own annexation page also points out two local details: street lighting there is provided by CoServ, and the Castle Hills HOA handles architectural review through its own committee. If a new system means a larger condenser or a new spot for it, check with the HOA first.

Gas heat and safety in Lewisville

Atmos Energy's Mid-Tex Division supplies natural gas in Lewisville; the city council considered its latest rate settlement with Atmos in September 2026. Many Lewisville homes have a gas furnace in the attic or a hall closet paired with an outdoor AC unit, while others use an electric heat pump. When you call, say which you have, because that helps the company you are connected with send someone ready for it.
