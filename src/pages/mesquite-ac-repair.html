Mesquite: a railroad depot that turned into a suburb

The Texas and Pacific Railway set up Mesquite as a depot town in 1873, and it became a city in 1887. For most of the next sixty years it stayed a farm town: the 1950 census counted 1,696 residents. Then the subdivisions arrived. Mesquite reached 27,526 people in 1960, 55,131 in 1970 and 101,484 in 1990. By 2020 the count stood at 150,108, with about 150,700 estimated for 2025.

Two local landmarks date from those growth years. The Mesquite Championship Rodeo started in 1946 and gave the city its official title, the Rodeo Capital of Texas. Town East Mall opened in 1971. Today most Mesquite households own their homes, about 63 percent according to the Census Bureau, and the median home value is roughly $245,700, lower than the Census Bureau figures for Garland, Irving or Richardson. A paid-off or long-held house is often worth a careful repair decision rather than a rushed one.

Repair or replace? How the age of a Mesquite house shapes the answer

A rough guide only. Check the data plate on your outdoor unit for its manufacture date and refrigerant type, and remember many homes have had their equipment swapped.

Usually worth a repair

A system under about 12 years old on R-410A with a single failed part: a capacitor, a contactor, a fan motor, a clogged drain or a thermostat problem. These are routine fixes for a good technician.

Time to get a replacement quote

A system that still runs R-22, which the EPA ended production of in 2020, or any unit with a failed compressor, a leaking coil or several breakdowns in two summers.

Look past the outdoor unit

In a house built in the 1970s or 1980s, the ducts may be original. Cracked or disconnected runs can make a healthy system feel weak, so ask for the ducts to be inspected too.

Ask for both numbers

The most useful visit ends with two written prices: the repair, and a properly sized replacement. Then you decide with real figures instead of pressure.

Permits work a little differently in Mesquite

The City of Mesquite lists AC and heating work among the jobs that need a permit. Before anyone can apply, they must hold a Professional License with the city, which is Mesquite's term for its registration. A mechanical contractor registers by providing a driver's license and its state mechanical license, and there is no fee for mechanical, electrical or plumbing contractors. Applications go through the city's Citizen Self Service portal, and Building Inspection can be reached at 972-216-6212.

That setup gives you an easy way to check a company. Ask whether it holds a current Professional License with Mesquite and who will pull the permit. If the answer is that you should pull it yourself, ask why: the city encourages homeowners to have their contractor obtain permits, because the person who signs for the permit is the one answering for the work.

A furnace that sat idle all summer should be checked before the first cold snap, not during it. Signs it needs attention include a burning smell that lasts more than the first few minutes, a burner that lights and shuts off repeatedly, or a yellow flame where it used to be blue. Never take the panel off a gas furnace to look inside. If you smell gas, leave the house and call from outside.

When a storm hits a Mesquite neighborhood

Mesquite sits near the southern end of Tornado Alley, and even a weak tornado can do real harm. On the morning of March 9, 2019, the National Weather Service confirmed an EF0 tornado with winds of 75 to 85 mph near Allen Drive, close to Interstate 635. Local news reported roofs torn off houses, broken windows and downed fences, and one family found its garage gone.

Wind like that throws branches and fence boards into outdoor units, and hail can flatten condenser fins. After any storm, walk around the unit before running the system. If the cabinet is dented, the copper lines look bent or the unit has shifted off its pad, keep it off and describe the damage when you call. Take photos first, in case your homeowners insurance asks for them.

Quick checks for a Mesquite system that will not cool

  • Thermostat: set to Cool and below room temperature, with good batteries.
  • Breaker: one reset if the AC breaker has tripped, no more.
  • Filter: replace it if you cannot see light through it.
  • Outside: keep at least a couple of feet clear around the condenser.
  • Drain: an overflow pan holding water usually means a clogged condensate line; say so on the call.
