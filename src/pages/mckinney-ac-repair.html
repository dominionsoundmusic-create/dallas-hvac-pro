McKinney: an old county seat that became a big city fast

McKinney has been the Collin County seat since the 1840s, and the 1876 courthouse still stands on the downtown square, now home to the McKinney Performing Arts Center. For more than a century the town stayed small. The census counted 15,193 people in 1970 and 21,283 in 1990. Then the subdivisions arrived: 54,369 residents in 2000, 131,117 in 2010 and 195,308 in 2020. The Census Bureau estimated about 236,000 by mid 2025, and it ranked McKinney among the fastest growing large cities in the country in the 2010s.

That history leaves two very different kinds of houses. Around the square sit homes built long before central air, which got their ducts squeezed into attics and crawl spaces decades later. Out toward Stonebridge Ranch, Craig Ranch and the newer areas north and west, most homes were built with central systems from day one, and many of those original systems are now old enough to need real attention. A good technician asks which kind of house you have before suggesting anything.

Which utility serves your McKinney address

McKinney sits on more than one utility map. Electricity guides list Oncor as the delivery utility for McKinney, which means you choose a retail provider and Oncor owns the poles, wires and meter. But the city's own energy report says its facilities buy electricity from a retail provider and also from CoServ and Grayson-Collin Electric Cooperative, two member-owned co-ops. Gas is split too: the city buys natural gas from both Atmos Energy and CoServ.

So a McKinney neighbor across a road may have a different electric company and a different gas company than you do. Before a storm or a summer outage, look at the top of each bill and save the outage and emergency numbers in your phone. None of these utilities work on air conditioners or furnaces, but knowing which one you have saves time when you are trying to decide whether the problem is the power or the system.

  • Nothing in the house has power. Report the outage to the utility on your bill. Switching the thermostat off until the power has been back for a while spares the compressor a rough restart.
  • Lights work, AC does not. Check the thermostat mode, setting and batteries, then look for one tripped breaker for the indoor or outdoor unit and reset it once.
  • The breaker trips again. Leave it off and call. A repeat trip points to a fault that needs a technician.
  • Cold air is weak or warm. Check for a clogged filter and leaves or grass packed against the outdoor coil. If you see ice on the copper lines, switch the system off and call.

Blackland clay and the outdoor unit

McKinney lies in the Texas Blackland Prairie, the belt of dark clay soil that runs from the Red River through Dallas-Fort Worth. These clays shrink and crack in dry weather and swell when they soak up rain. Over a few seasons that movement can tilt a condenser pad, pull on the copper line set where it enters the wall and change the slope of the condensate drain so water backs up into the indoor unit. A careful technician checks that the outdoor unit is level and that the drain still runs downhill, because a drain that has lost its slope will keep clogging however many times it is flushed.

Before you replace a system in McKinney

The City of McKinney publishes a list of home repairs that need no permit. For heating and cooling it covers only minor work, such as changing filters and fixing small leaks without moving piping. A full replacement is not on that list, and the city handles permits and inspections through its Citizen Self-Service portal. Ask that the permit appear on the written quote, and keep the inspection record with your house papers.

  • A load calculation. A downtown home with new windows or a Craig Ranch home with a finished bonus room may need a different size than the old unit.
  • The ducts. In older McKinney homes, a new system on old ducts can still lose cool air into the attic.
  • The right incentives. If Oncor delivers your power, ask whether a project qualifies for Oncor's Home Energy Efficiency program, which works through approved service providers. Co-op members should ask CoServ or Grayson-Collin directly.
  • The license number. Look it up yourself before you sign.
