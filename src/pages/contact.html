What to say when you call

You do not need technical words. Short and specific is perfect. Have these ready:

  1. Cooling or heating. The first thing the assistant asks.
  2. Your name and a callback number. Give a number you will answer, in case the company has to call you back.
  3. Your city or ZIP code. This is how the call reaches a company that works in your part of DFW.
  4. What the system is doing. For example: "The AC runs but the air is warm, started this afternoon." "Water is dripping through the hallway ceiling below the attic unit." "The furnace clicks but never lights."

If it is easy to see, the brand and age on the label of the outdoor unit or furnace help the company prepare. Before you call, it is also worth checking the thermostat setting and batteries and the air filter; the AC repair page lists the safe checks.

Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm? Do this first

Gas smell: leave the house right away without switching lights or appliances on or off. From a safe distance, call 911 and your gas utility. Atmos Energy, which serves the Dallas and Fort Worth area, lists its 24-hour emergency number as 866-322-8667.

Carbon monoxide alarm: get everyone out of the house and call 911. Carbon monoxide has no color or smell, and a malfunctioning furnace is one possible source. Do not run the furnace again until a qualified technician has inspected it.

Heat illness: if someone in a hot house is confused, faint or very hot to the touch, call 911 before you call about the air conditioner.

Questions about a job that is already done

The line does not do the work or hold the paperwork, so questions about a visit, an invoice or a warranty go to the company you hired. Its name, phone number and, if you asked for it, its TDLR license number should be on the quote or invoice.
