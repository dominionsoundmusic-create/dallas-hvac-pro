Carrollton: a city that boomed in the 1970s

The Handbook of Texas describes Carrollton as a farming and railroad town that became known for "grain and gravel." It had only 1,610 residents in 1950. Then the Sun Belt boom arrived. The population hit 13,855 in 1970 and jumped 193 percent to 40,595 by 1980, and the Handbook notes that nearly three quarters of the city's housing at that time had been built during that one decade. The city kept growing, to 82,169 in 1990 and 133,434 in the 2020 Census.

For an air conditioner, that history is the most important local fact. A house finished in 1975 has probably been through several outdoor units by now, yet it may still have the duct layout, return grille size and attic insulation its builder chose half a century ago. A good technician looks at the whole system, not just the box in the yard, because a new condenser cannot make up for a return that is too small or ducts that leak into a hot attic.

Three counties, one set of utilities

Carrollton is one of the few DFW cities split across three counties: Dallas, Denton and Collin. That split confuses new residents, but it does not change the utilities. Oncor delivers electricity across Carrollton, which means you choose a retail electric provider for your bill while Oncor maintains the wires and handles outages. Atmos Energy operates the natural gas system; local news coverage of a January 2024 house explosion on Highland Drive, caused by gas that leaked into a sewer line, quoted the fire chief on the leak that Atmos crews found.

Here is how to tell which kind of problem you have before calling anyone:

  • Nothing in the house has power. It is an outage. Report it to Oncor, and set the thermostat to Off until power comes back steady.
  • Lights work but the AC is silent. Check the thermostat mode and batteries, then look for a tripped breaker and reset it one time only.
  • The breaker trips again. Leave it off. A repeat trip points to an electrical fault inside the equipment that a technician needs to find.
  • Air blows but it is warm. Check for a clogged filter and make sure the outdoor unit is not buried in leaves or grass clippings. If you see ice on the copper lines, shut the system off and call.

Hail, heat and the outdoor unit

Carrollton has seen what a North Texas hailstorm can do. In the early hours of June 6, 2018, baseball-size hail hit Carrollton, Coppell and Arlington. CBS Texas reported heavy property damage along Hebron Parkway and State Highway 121 on the city's west side, and reporters found smashed windows across blocks between I-35E and the Sam Rayburn Tollway. The same report cited an Insurance Council of Texas estimate of about $425 million in insured losses from that storm.

Hail is hard on condensers. The thin aluminum fins on the coil fold over when they are struck, and a badly flattened coil cannot shed heat, so the system runs longer and the head pressure climbs. After a storm, look at the outdoor unit from a few feet away. If the fins look crushed or the fan guard is bent, have a company inspect it before the next hot stretch, and photograph the damage first in case your homeowner's policy covers it.

Before you replace a system in Carrollton

  • Permit. Carrollton charges mechanical permits by job value, $4 per $1,000 with a $75 minimum, and issues them through the CityServe portal. Contractors also register with the city.
  • Sizing. Ask for a load calculation. A 1970s house with new windows and attic insulation may need a smaller unit than the one it has.
  • Ducts. Ask whether the ducts will be tested, sealed or replaced, and get that line item in writing.
  • Refrigerant. New equipment now uses lower impact refrigerants such as R-454B or R-32. In May 2026 the EPA finalized a change that lets existing R-410A equipment keep being installed while supply lasts, so ask which you are getting and why.
  • License. Get the TDLR license number on the quote and look it up before signing.
